# Team Astralis lunar portfolio scene

This viewer uses the Orbit, Moon Map, Surface, camera transition and landing suggestion code from [Lunar Landing Site Navigator v1.5.1](https://github.com/Debaprashad-Bhowmik/project-astralis/tree/v1.5.1). The portfolio supplies mission and scene controls through same-origin messages. The rendering window contains the canvases and map markers.

The default is Patch 1 on 25 December 2026 at 00:00 UTC. The bundled SPICE ephemeris covers 2024–2030. The Moon Map projects the Sun and Earth from its current camera using the same perspective and occultation as Orbit. The landing worker screens the original 6,685-patch inventory over the requested mission and evaluates all 39 curated locations copied from the original navigator's catalog. Clicking a marker or any point on the Moon opens its surface. Surface terrain loads on demand from the bundled global LOLA tiles, higher-resolution south-polar grids, and site-specific datasets when available. Foreground rocks and the surface texture retain their illustrative treatment.

The globe uses the original 4K LROC texture as its base image. Orbit zoom uses the portfolio controls or Ctrl + scroll so regular scrolling continues through the page. See the team's [source register](https://github.com/Debaprashad-Bhowmik/project-astralis/blob/v1.5.1/docs/submission-sources.md) and [third-party notices](https://github.com/Debaprashad-Bhowmik/project-astralis/blob/v1.5.1/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md) for scientific, imagery, and media credits.

The background in Orbit, Moon Map and Surface uses the celestial-coordinate edition of [Deep Star Maps 2020](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851/) by Ernie Wright at NASA Goddard's Scientific Visualization Studio, based on Hipparcos, Tycho-2 and Gaia. The original 4K EXR was tone-mapped and compressed to WebP for the viewer. The bundled JPL SPICE ephemeris and each camera's orientation place the sky in J2000 equatorial coordinates. Apparent sky brightness is a display exposure that responds to sunlit foreground and solar glare. The visual lighting includes approximate Earthshine from a diffuse Earth and the existing direct solar shadows. These display effects do not enter the landing-site scores.
